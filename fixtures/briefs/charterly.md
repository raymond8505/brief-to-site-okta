# Fixture brief — Charterly

> **Test case.** A five-page brief for a claims-heavy category. Exercises two things the shorter
> fixtures don't: a **wider page set** (Home / How it works / Pricing / About / Contact), which
> pushes past the Home/About/Contact default into pricing-tier and process compositions; and the
> **guardrail path** on a product whose real-world equivalents advertise prices, filing times, and
> customer counts — none of which this brief supplies, so every figure on the finished site must be
> staged placeholder-grade and surfaced at HITL.
>
> Structurally inspired by the online business-formation category (ownr.co and its peers). Charterly
> is fictional and is **not** a reproduction of any real company's copy, pricing, or design.

## What is this?

Charterly registers and incorporates small Canadian businesses online, then keeps them compliant
year after year. It's two things joined at the hip: a **guided formation flow** that turns a founder's
answers into a filed sole proprietorship or corporation, and an **ongoing compliance subscription**
that holds the corporate records afterward — the digital minute book, the annual filings, changes to
directors and officers, and the standard agreements a small company actually needs.

The pitch is the contrast: the traditional route is a lawyer's retainer, a few weeks of back-and-forth,
and a binder that goes stale in a drawer. Charterly is one sitting, a price you can see before you
start, and records that stay current because the platform is the one keeping them.

## Audience

Two groups, arriving from opposite directions.

**First-time founders** who have the business figured out and are stalled on the legal step. It's the
part that feels like it needs a professional, and they don't know what they don't know. They want it
done correctly, quickly, and without discovering a fee at the end.

**Existing owners** already paying someone hourly for routine annual filings and record-keeping, who
suspect that work is more clerical than legal. They're not shopping for advice; they're shopping for
the same outcome at a predictable cost.

Both care about the same three things: how fast it's real, what it costs before they commit, and
whether it was done right.

## Key messages

- **Official in an afternoon, not a month.** The formation flow is one sitting; the filing goes out
  the same day.
- **One flow, then it keeps itself current.** Registration is the start, not the product — the
  durable value is that the minute book, the filings, and the officer records stay up to date without
  anyone remembering to do it.
- **A fixed price you can see before you start.** No hourly meter, no surprise at the end. What's
  included at each level is on the page.
- **Built by people who know the filings.** Real expertise in the process, delivered as software
  instead of billable hours.

## Pages

- **Home** — the full arc. Hook on making it official → the two-path choice (register as a sole
  proprietor vs. incorporate) so visitors self-select immediately → how it works, condensed to the
  numbered flow → why it holds up (the compliance features that outlast signup) → proof from owners
  who've done it → a pricing teaser that frames the two-part cost → FAQ for the objections that stop
  people (is this legal advice? what if I'm not sure which entity?) → close. Primary CTA: "Get
  started" → /pricing.
- **How it works** — the numbered flow as the page's spine: choose your structure → answer the
  guided questions → we file with the registry → your documents arrive → manage everything from one
  dashboard. Then two follow-on sections: what you have on day one, and what happens every year after
  (the part people don't think about when they're buying). CTA: "See pricing" → /pricing.
- **Pricing** — the formation options side by side first (the one-time decision), then the annual
  compliance tiers, then a clear breakdown of what's included at each level, then an FAQ covering
  what costs extra and what government fees are separate. CTA: "Talk to us" → /contact.
- **About** — why the company exists: the legal step is the one that stalls people, and it shouldn't.
  What stays constant (fixed pricing, records that stay current, plain language), and who's behind
  it. CTA: "Get in touch" → /contact.
- **Contact** — the inquiry form, framed for both audiences (starting fresh vs. moving an existing
  corporation over), plus a short pre-sales FAQ.

CTAs point only at real pages in this set: "Get started" / "See pricing" → /pricing, "Talk to us" /
"Get in touch" → /contact, "How it works" → /how-it-works.

## Visual direction

Modern and confident, not stuffy-legal. The violet seed (`#5f3dc8`) should read like a well-made
financial product rather than a law office — this is software that happens to do serious paperwork.
Complementary harmony puts the derived accents roughly at gold and green off the violet: use the
**gold** note where something is official or sealed (a completed step, a document badge) and the
**green** where something is progressing or done. Generous corner radius — a product about
intimidating paperwork should feel approachable at a glance.

Imagery: competent and calm. A clean desk with one signed document, an owner in their actual shop,
a dashboard on a laptop screen. Real small businesses at human scale — not boardrooms, not
handshake stock photography, not gavels or scales of justice.

Iconography (lucide): `FileCheck` / `Stamp` / `BadgeCheck` for official-and-filed, `ShieldCheck` for
compliance, `CalendarCheck` for the annual rhythm, `Building2` for the corporation itself,
`ArrowRight` for the flow between steps, `Mail` for contact. Motifs: stamped and sealed shapes,
numbered progress steps, subtle document edges and corners.

## Notes

- Brand colour: violet `#5f3dc8` (primary). Harmony: `complementary` (accents derived). Radius
  preset: `rounded`.
- **No checkable specifics are supplied, deliberately.** This brief contains no prices, no filing
  turnaround times, no customer counts, no partner or bank names, and no named customers. Any figure
  the assembled site needs — tier prices, "N businesses registered", testimonial attributions — is
  **staged placeholder-grade** and must be listed for operator sign-off at HITL. That is the point of
  this fixture, not an omission to fill in.
- Charterly is fictional. The copy makes no legal, regulatory, or professional-services claims, and
  should avoid implying the platform gives legal advice.
